The AI kit for tax offices

In June 2026, the IRS Office of Professional Responsibility said that a tax firm using AI needs internal policies covering four things: training staff, handling client data securely, checking AI's accuracy, and vetting outside AI tools. The kit gives an office one piece for each. It isn't tax, legal or compliance advice, and the office's security plan stays its own.

  • Handling client data: Clotr Before a client's Social Security number or ITIN, bank or routing number, date of birth or address, or a name on the office's own list goes into a browser AI chat, Clotr warns, or asks first. It doesn't make an AI tool secure, and the person can still choose to send.
  • Staff training: an interactive walkthrough Each person takes it inside Clotr, self-paced, about 15 to 20 minutes, offline, in English or Spanish (coming in Clotr's next update). Each person prints a completion page with their name and the date.
  • Vetting AI tools: a one-page checklist The answers for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and Perplexity on training, how long chats are kept, business plans and where the settings are, each linked to the company's own help page and dated.
  • Accuracy checks: a review step A fill-in clause for the office's written security plan, naming who checks AI's facts, figures and citations before work goes to a client or the IRS. Clotr doesn't check what an AI writes; people do.
