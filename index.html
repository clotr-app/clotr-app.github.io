Clotr Antibody: when someone asks for something

Most scams end with a request: a code, your card's numbers, gift cards, or a command to paste into your computer. Clotr Antibody will be there at that moment, on your computer.

  • In the moment The warning tells you who's really asking. A code your bank texts you is for typing into your bank's own app or website, never into a chat or to someone who messaged you.
  • A second look Paste a message you got and see the warning signs Clotr knows. It will never tell you a message is fine, because no list knows every scam.
  • Afterwards For 30 days after a scam, Clotr will ask before money and ID numbers go out. Scammers often come back.
  • Practice Six made-up scam messages to answer, checked by the real engine.

Honest about what it does

  • Nothing leaves your device. No servers, no analytics, no accounts. A test checks it every time the code changes.

  • It never saves what you typed. Only what kind of thing it found, where, when, and what you did about it.

  • Tested, not just promised. I hid 6,200 personal details in 10,000 made-up messages. This build caught 5,027 of them, with 30 false alarms on the 3,800 that had nothing to catch. Messages typed on a phone are messier: there it caught 205 of 310. Real life is messier still, so if it misses something or warns about nothing, tell me.

  • No AI inside, and open code. Plain rules that anyone can read. Free for people, always.
