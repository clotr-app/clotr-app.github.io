Clotr for teams

Clotr is free at work too. An administrator can set its policy through supported browser management; the organization still uses the same free Clotr as everyone else. Clotr reports no prompts or findings to the organization.

What an organization never sees

  • What your people type
  • What Clotr caught
  • When

Clotr does not report prompts, findings, or staff activity to the organization, and it has no admin dashboard.

Roll it out

Ready-made presets for developer credentials, configured client/matter details, clinics and tax offices, plus a printable page that shows the policy Clotr read on that computer. A team can add its own kinds of detail, like a matter number, and when its policy asks before sending, attached files wait for an answer too.

A team's own watch words, formats and labels arrive through its browser or device-management policy, the same way IT sets anything else. Clotr turns watch words into fingerprints before it matches them, but the policy itself stays readable to whoever manages it, and each computer may keep a readable copy.

Labels show up in warnings and on the policy page, which can also show the watch words when someone asks it to. So keep labels general and use made-up examples, unless your office has decided real client names are fine there.

Why teams matter to Clotr

I'm building Clotr toward a privacy engine people can rely on everywhere they type, and teams are what drive that work. When an office needs Clotr to catch something new, it lands in the engine everyone gets for free. Clotr is funded by me personally, and anything teams contribute goes into development. Teamwork lifts us all.
